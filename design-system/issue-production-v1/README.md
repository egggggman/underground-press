# Issue Production System v1

This system turns approved editorial copy into repeatable Underground Press
pages without reducing the newspaper to interchangeable boxes. The approved
Page One remains the Issue 001 execution benchmark; this system encodes its
visual grammar rather than copying its front-page composition.

## Separation of concerns

- **Copy files** contain reporting and remain layout-independent.
- **Page manifests** declare editorial roles, hierarchy, assets, discoveries,
  and occupied regions.
- **Page builders** art-direct each department using shared press primitives.
- **Validation** checks the manifest, source assets, visual-role coverage, and
  density before a proof can be reviewed.
- **Rendered proofs** are disposable outputs rebuilt from tracked sources.

## Non-negotiable grammar

Every full editorial page requires:

1. one dominant story and one dominant integrated image or illustration;
2. at least eight visibly distinct editorial modules or pieces of furniture;
3. at least three typographic scales and two non-body display treatments;
4. one utility/service route and one close-reading discovery;
5. at least two provenance-bearing spot objects or illustrations;
6. deterministic paper tooth, ink wear, and restrained registration behavior;
7. occupied visual area of at least 82% inside the safe page;
8. no accidental empty rectangle larger than 18% of the safe page;
9. the exact motto and anonymous newsroom convention;
10. explicit comparison against both the Golden Image and locked Page One.

Passing the contract does not automatically approve a page. It prevents known
failure modes—clean infographic layouts, cloned boxes, missing art, sparse
pages, and surface-only distress—from reaching editorial review.

## Certification sequence

Page 2 is the certification page. The system becomes production-approved only
after Page 2 looks physically printed beside the benchmark at identical scale.
Page 3 is then rebuilt from the same primitives without copying Page 2's layout.
Only after both pass may later departments use the system.
