# City Beat Department Guide

## Purpose and authority

City Beat turns local reporting into a page neighbors can use quickly and remember slowly. This guide governs editorial application within the approved City Beat template; it does not authorize changes to that template, approved artwork, Puzzle Dojo materials, or production component assets.

Apply it beneath the project's controlling references: `docs/STYLE_BIBLE.md`, `docs/EDITORIAL_PHILOSOPHY.md`, `docs/NEWSROOM_CHARTER.md`, `docs/CANON.md`, `world/WORLD_BIBLE.md`, and `world/VOICE.md`. The approved Issue #1 front page remains the visual Gold Standard.

## Layout philosophy

Build the page like a neighborhood desk assembled from reliable reporting and handled paper. The result may feel found, repaired, reused, improved, and loved, but never accidental.

- **Report first.** The lead and utility modules establish the grid; collage details respond to them.
- **Three-story minimum.** Every page carries a primary story, a secondary story, and a discovery.
- **Fast path, slow rewards.** A hurried reader finds weather, transit, and dates immediately. A careful reader finds callbacks, marginalia, and material clues.
- **Modular rhythm.** Strong rules and stable recurring positions make the department familiar across issues while story scale changes.
- **Controlled imperfection.** Slight rotation, wear, ink bleed, and registration drift belong at the surface. Baselines, reading order, and factual labels remain disciplined.
- **Sliding Time.** Preserve the 1991-rooted present through objects, habits, print culture, and pacing. Use exact dates only when the issue requires them, and do not let an incidental timestamp freeze the wider world.

## Visual hierarchy

Use four tiers:

1. **Department and lead:** City Beat slug, lead headline, dek, and primary image/evidence element.
2. **Urgent utility:** Weather and Transit Watch, distinguished through compact fields, rules, and restrained oxblood or sewer-green emphasis.
3. **Community service:** Calendar and Neighborhood Notes, grouped consistently and headed clearly.
4. **Discovery:** a stamp, clipped note, repair, doodle, or found item that rewards attention but carries no essential instruction.

At thumbnail size, the lead headline and utility rail should remain distinguishable. At print size, no texture, rotation, or narrow measure may compromise comfortable body reading. Use white space as structure before filling it with ephemera.

## Found-object collage

The collage vocabulary comes from the neighborhood itself: clipped newsprint, notebook scraps, community flyers, ferry or service tickets, utility tags, inspection stickers, tape, staples, red pencil, rubber stamps, harbor rope, weathered wood, business cards, coupons, and restrained pizza or coffee traces.

Every object needs one job:

- establish source or provenance;
- separate a recurring module;
- locate the story in Portland's working, weathered environment;
- carry a caption, correction, or editor's note;
- create a small discovery or continuity reward.

Do not use decoration to simulate reporting. Never place required dates, safety guidance, route alternatives, attribution, or correction language on a low-contrast or partially obscured object. Limit competing novelty objects; one memorable discovery is stronger than a pile of unrelated scraps.

Use TMNT spirit as a quiet accent—shell geometry, skate wear, pizza grease, arcade marks, comic marginalia—not as constant character branding. The publication is a neighborhood newspaper first.

## Typography and color

Use the approved template and design-system tokens as the implementation source. Licensed font files do not belong in the repository.

- **Lead headlines:** approved condensed display face; decisive line breaks; no novelty distortion.
- **Module heads and labels:** approved condensed or utility sans treatment; short, consistent, and easy to scan.
- **Body and briefs:** approved editorial serif; readable measures, leading, and contrast.
- **Metadata:** approved utility face for times, routes, verification labels, and issue furniture.
- **Handwriting/typewriter accents:** reserved for genuine marginalia, source notes, or found objects; never substitute them for body copy.

Warm newsprint is the ground. Oxblood signals alerts and strong rules; sewer green supports civic utility fields; mustard calls attention sparingly. Black remains the reporting workhorse. Registration drift and ink variation must be restrained and never applied to small essential text.

## Recurring components

### Lead Local Story

One reported change or concern anchors the page. It needs a useful question, human sources, locally specific observations, and a takeaway that matters to a neighbor. A diagram, map-like illustration, document clipping, or photo may support it, but fictionalized geography must never masquerade as a precise municipal map.

### Weather

Combine verified forecast facts with one appropriate approved inventory note. Separate measured or sourced data from atmospheric copy. Weather should explain movement and daily consequences above and below Portland, not merely provide mood.

### Transit Watch

Lead with impact: what is delayed, closed, obstructed, or changed; where; when; and what the reader can do. Use clear category labels. Temporary conditions do not establish permanent geography.

### Community Calendar

Make dates and times the fastest facts to find. Every entry requires a verified organizer, location, and accessibility note where applicable. The tone welcomes newcomers and does not assume prior continuity.

### Neighborhood Notes

Use short reported briefs for repairs, acts of care, business-adjacent observations, small changes, and carefully labeled leads. Distinguish reporting from memory, rumor, legend, advertising, and opinion. A callback should delight returning readers while remaining intelligible to new ones.

## Living Masthead

The masthead's typography, logo, placement, color, and composition are immutable. The world may interact with it only through a tiny, issue-specific detail approved by the art director: a trace of weather, a caught leaf, a repair, a coffee ring, or another found-object clue.

Rules for a living detail:

- occupy no more than 5% of the masthead area;
- remain removable and subordinate;
- never reduce recognition or readability;
- never move, redraw, recolor, or replace the logo or type;
- connect to the issue's reported season or story rather than random decoration.

For Issue #1 City Beat, a faint fog-softened edge or small condensation mark may be explored only if it can be added without modifying the approved masthead asset itself.

## Portland identity and voice

Write with local confidence: salt air, rain-dark brick, cold iron, gulls, ferry horns, chains, pipes, radiators, tires on wet streets, and warm counters can sharpen a scene when reporting supports them. Choose a few exact details rather than a catalog. The waterfront is working and inhabited, not postcard scenery.

The tone is observant, plainspoken, scrappy, affectionate, and alert to the strange. Humor comes from character and familiarity, never cruelty. Do not invent claims of real-world historical or geographic accuracy. The underground is an overlapping civic habitat of storm drains, utility tunnels, abandoned waterfront infrastructure, forgotten basements, and service corridors—not a literal map of Portland infrastructure.

## Visual identity and motto

City Beat belongs to a publication made for neighbors above and below the city. The motto, *For Those Who Found Their Way.*, should appear as stable publication furniture—not a headline gimmick—and should reinforce welcome, belonging, and discovery.

Before approval, ask:

> Would this page feel like it came off the same press as the approved front page?

Then ask:

> Can a neighbor find what they need before the collage asks to be admired?

Both answers must be yes.

## Department preflight

- Preserve all locked templates, artwork, component assets, and Puzzle Dojo systems.
- Confirm the lead, secondary story, and discovery.
- Verify utility data and distinguish it from inventory atmosphere.
- Resolve all placeholders and label facts, memories, rumors, legends, opinions, and ads correctly.
- Check business facts and recurring details against canon.
- Confirm typography and colors use approved tokens and roles.
- Test hierarchy at thumbnail size and readability at final print size.
- Confirm collage objects have an editorial job and obscure nothing essential.
- Confirm any Living Masthead detail follows the 5% and immutability rules.
- Confirm *For Those Who Found Their Way.* is exact and legible.
