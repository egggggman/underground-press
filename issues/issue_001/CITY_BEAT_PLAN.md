# Issue 001 City Beat Plan

**Status:** Editorial and production plan  
**Department:** City Beat  
**Motto:** *For Those Who Found Their Way.*

## Editorial objective

Make City Beat the useful, observant center of Issue #1: a page where neighbors above and below Portland can understand what changed, what may slow them down, where to gather, and which small details deserve remembering. Reporting should be plainspoken, locally textured, and alert to strangeness without turning residents into scenery.

The page must give a first-time reader a complete visit while establishing modules that can accumulate continuity under the Sliding Time model. Dates, conditions, schedules, quotations, and operational details remain issue-specific and must be verified at press time; durable canon is not created by an unverified listing.

## Page architecture

The approved City Beat production template is preserved. This plan assigns editorial roles to its existing regions and does not authorize redesigning the template or modifying production components.

1. **Living Masthead zone:** use the approved masthead composition, typography, logo, and color unchanged. If the issue art director approves a living detail, keep it seasonal, removable, and below 5% of the masthead area.
2. **Lead local story:** dominant headline, dek, byline, reported body, and one useful evidence element. It supplies the page's primary story.
3. **Utility rail:** Weather and Transit Watch, placed for fast scanning. These modules supply immediate neighbor value.
4. **Community rail:** Community Calendar and Neighborhood Notes. Together they provide the secondary story and a welcoming route into the city.
5. **Discovery pocket:** one small found item, margin note, or reported detail that rewards close reading without carrying essential facts.
6. **Footer furniture:** issue marker, department slug, continuation directions if needed, and the motto, *For Those Who Found Their Way.*

Reading order must remain obvious at arm's length: lead story, urgent utility, community entries, then discovery. No collage layer may interrupt that path.

## Issue #1 editorial package

### Lead local story

**Working slug:** `CITY-LEAD-001`  
**Working headline:** *When the Fog Moves In, the Block Listens*  
**Editorial question:** How do neighbors, workers, and underground travelers adjust when waterfront fog shortens sightlines but carries bells, horns, and pipe sounds farther?

Report this as a service story grounded in daily routines, not as a supernatural claim or a piece of tourism copy. Seek at least three voices: one waterfront worker or resident, one person who regularly moves between aboveground and underground routes, and one organizer or steward responsible for access or safety. Confirm every quotation, location, route condition, and safety instruction before publication.

Suggested evidence element: a small, clearly illustrative sound-and-sightline diagram based on reported observations. Do not imply a precise municipal map.

### Weather

Use approved Editorial Inventory item `WTH-001` as the atmospheric base:

> Fog may erase the far end of the block before breakfast. Use bells, lamps, and patience.

Pair it with verified issue-day forecast data. Label forecast values separately from the inventory's lived-conditions copy. If fog is not supported by the verified forecast or season at lock, select another approved inventory item that matches conditions and update the lead-story framing rather than forcing the package.

### Transit Watch

Reserve one urgent item and up to two briefs from `content/transit_watch/inventory.json`. Prefer categories that complement the verified conditions: Weather, Access, Delay, or Courtesy. Each published brief must replace inventory placeholders with a confirmed route or access point, time window, cause where publishable, and practical alternative. Never present underground geography as an exact real-world municipal map.

### Community Calendar

Provisional selections from the approved inventory:

- `CAL-030`, **Fog-safety talk** — strongest service connection to the lead package.
- `CAL-007`, **Storm-drain cleanup** — community stewardship with above/below relevance.
- `CAL-018`, **Lantern repair hour** — useful, era-appropriate, and visually compatible with the page.
- `CAL-009`, **Local history listening hour** — invites community memory without treating testimony as objective chronology.

Before publication, confirm each date, time, organizer, accessibility note, and location. Replace any item that cannot be verified with another approved, seasonally appropriate inventory entry.

### Business mentions

Business references are editorial, never filler or disguised advertising.

- `BSP-004`, **Tony's Donuts / “Before the City Is Ready”** may support a short Neighborhood Note about early-shift routines after current operational details and any quotation are verified.
- `BSP-002`, **Quality Shop / “Wrapped for the Walk Home”** is held as the secondary option if reporting naturally connects lunch traffic or a walk-home route to the package.

Keep permanent facts intact: Tony's Donuts is a recurring breakfast shop with rotating donut specials; Quality Shop is the Shamos family neighborhood institution known for outstanding Italian sandwiches. Do not invent specials, prices, hours, or endorsements. Paid placement must be labeled and handled outside editorial copy.

### Neighborhood Notes

Commission three to five briefs of 35–70 words. The mix should include:

- one verified access, repair, or stewardship note;
- one small act by an everyday neighbor;
- one business-adjacent observation that passes the editorial-versus-advertising test;
- one continuity seed that remains understandable without prior issues;
- optionally, one clearly labeled rumor lead for later reporting.

Each note needs a source, verification status, district or location label when useful, and a check against the canon register. Humor should come from observation and familiarity, never cruelty.

## Recurring modules

| Module | Reader promise | Typical size | Required fields | Continuity rule |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Lead Local Story | Explain one consequential local change through people and useful facts. | 650–900 words | Headline, dek, byline, sources, body, evidence element | Follow-ups may recall prior reporting, but every installment must stand alone. |
| Weather | Tell neighbors what conditions mean for movement and daily life. | 45–80 words | Verified forecast, inventory note, timestamp/source | Conditions are issue-specific; atmospheric language does not establish history. |
| Transit Watch | Identify a disruption and the safest practical response. | 35–65 words each | Category, route/access point, window, impact, alternative, verification | Never silently turn a temporary route condition into permanent geography. |
| Community Calendar | Help readers find a welcoming, verified gathering. | 25–45 words each | Event, date, time, organizer, location, accessibility, verification | Events enter continuity only after publication and approval. |
| Neighborhood Notes | Preserve useful small news and community texture. | 35–70 words each | Label, place, source, body, status | Label rumors, memories, ads, and reported facts distinctly. |

## Acceptance criteria

City Beat is accepted when:

1. The page is visibly part of the same 1991 Portland newspaper as the approved Issue #1 front page.
2. The approved City Beat template, production components, artwork, and Puzzle Dojo materials remain unchanged.
3. A clear lead local story, secondary community package, and close-reading discovery are present.
4. Weather, Transit Watch, Community Calendar, and Neighborhood Notes are scannable recurring modules with verified issue-specific details.
5. Inventory-derived material is traceable by ID, seasonally appropriate, and not published with unresolved placeholders.
6. Reporting respects the Style Bible, Editorial Philosophy, Portland canon, Sliding Time, and the distinction between history, memory, rumor, legend, opinion, and advertising.
7. Visual hierarchy remains readable; collage details deepen story and place without obscuring facts.
8. The Living Masthead retains its immutable identity and any approved living detail stays subtle and subordinate.
9. The tone is useful, curious, scrappy, affectionate, and alert to weirdness, and the motto appears exactly as *For Those Who Found Their Way.*
10. Markdown validation and `git diff --check` pass with no errors.
