# The Underground Press World Bible

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Authoritative  
**Setting decision:** LOCKED

This is the authoritative reference for the setting, continuity, and recurring world of every future issue of The Underground Press. When issue copy conflicts with this document or a canonical entry beneath `world/`, the World Bible wins unless a later locked decision explicitly changes it.

## Core premise

The Underground Press is a neighborhood newspaper documenting life beneath Portland, Maine in a TMNT-inspired world.

It is not merely a record of events. It is also a record of a community's memories.

History establishes the best-known chronology of what happened. Memory preserves how people experienced, retell, and understand what happened. Both are canonical in their own ways: history as established fact, and memory as an authentic part of community identity even when recollections differ. Legends and rumors remain canonical as stories in circulation, not as proof that their claims are true.

It is published from beneath Portland and covers the people, businesses, events, puzzles, local game stores, everyday heroes, small traditions, humor, and ongoing continuity of the community above and below street level.

## Canonical world rules

1. **Portland, Maine is the official city.**
2. **The newspaper is published from beneath Portland.**
3. **The underground consists of storm drains, utility tunnels, abandoned waterfront infrastructure, forgotten basements, and service corridors.**
4. **The newspaper aesthetic remains rooted in 1991.**
5. **Real Portland flavor is encouraged.**
6. **The TMNT-inspired universe remains intact.**
7. **The world is inspired by Portland, not constrained to perfectly replicate it.**

## Creative boundary

Treat Portland as Gotham treats New York: a recognizable source of identity, texture, weather, neighborhoods, waterfront energy, and local character—not a GIS map or a demand for documentary precision.

Do not invent Portland facts that require historical accuracy. Real places may inspire stories and recurring features, but fictionalization must be clear whenever precision would matter.

## The underground

The underground is an overlapping civic habitat rather than one continuous mapped sewer. Its usable spaces include:

- storm drains
- utility tunnels
- abandoned waterfront infrastructure
- forgotten basements
- service corridors

Connections may be secret, unstable, repurposed, blocked, repaired, or known only to particular residents. Geography should serve story continuity without pretending to replicate real municipal infrastructure.

## Era and technology

The visual and editorial present is rooted in 1991. Production choices, advertising rhythms, classifieds, community notices, and physical-media texture should support that era. The setting may bend reality in service of the TMNT-inspired universe, but it should not drift into generic contemporary digital culture without an explicit story reason.

## Editorial focus

The paper exists to celebrate:

- neighborhood businesses
- community events
- puzzles
- local game stores
- everyday heroes
- small traditions
- humor
- continuity

The tone is local, curious, scrappy, affectionate, and alert to weirdness.

## Permanent business canon

The permanent flagship businesses are:

- **The Crust Bucket** — recurring pizza advertiser; replaces Tony’s Pizza; rotating coupons
- **Quality Shop** — Shamos family neighborhood institution; outstanding Italian sandwiches
- **Great Lost Bear** — recurring restaurant/bar; legendary ranch; weekly specials
- **Tony’s Donuts** — recurring breakfast shop; rotating donut specials

See `businesses/FLAGSHIP_BUSINESSES.md` for the canonical entries and `docs/BUSINESS_BIBLE.md` for editorial usage.

## Continuity rules

1. Check the relevant `world/` category before introducing a recurring detail.
2. Add new durable facts after an issue is approved, not during speculative drafting.
3. Record significant events in `events/` and place them on `timeline/`.
4. Record recurring jokes only after they have earned continuity.
5. Prefer evocative consistency over false real-world precision.
6. A locked decision can be changed only by a later explicit locked decision.
7. Published issue snapshots remain immutable.
